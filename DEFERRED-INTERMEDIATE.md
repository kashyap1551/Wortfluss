# Deferred to an Intermediate tier

Entries encountered during A1/A2 extraction that can't be taught within
the Beginner constraint (nominative case, present tense, simple sentence
structure — PRD.md §8) without either forcing a fit that breaks the data
model or teaching grammar the app doesn't have a mechanic for yet. Each
was a deliberate skip, not an oversight. Revisit if/when an Intermediate
tier is actually built (PRD.md §8/§9) — not before.

**Count so far: 3.**

## mein, meine — "my" (possessive determiner)

**Source:** Netzwerk neu A1, Kapitel 1, section 3a.

**Why deferred:** Inflects by gender/case (mein vs. meine vs. meinen...),
which is exactly the case/ending-agreement complexity the `adjective`
type is deliberately kept in predicate position to avoid (ARCHITECTURE.md
§2). Doesn't fit `noun`, `verb`, or `phrase` either — it needs a real
possessive-determiner mechanic (agreement with the following noun's
gender/case), which doesn't exist yet. Its invariant forms still show up
as incidental flavor inside other words' sentences where grammatically
safe (e.g. "Ich bin dein Freund.") without being taught as their own
vocabulary — same treatment as ordinary function words.

## welche, welcher — "which" (interrogative determiner)

**Source:** Netzwerk neu A1, Kapitel 1, section 4a.

**Why deferred:** Same shape of problem as `mein/meine` — varies by the
gender/case of the noun it's asking about (welche vs. welcher vs.
welches...). No mechanic exists for a determiner whose correct form
depends on agreement rather than being fixed.

## verabschieden — "to say goodbye"

**Source:** Netzwerk neu A1, Kapitel 1, "kurz und klar" review section.

**Why deferred:** Reflexive verb — the glossary's own citation form omits
the reflexive pronoun ("verabschieden — to say goodbye"), but the actual
German is *sich verabschieden* ("Ich verabschiede mich."). Reflexive
verbs are an explicitly deferred category (they need a mechanic for the
reflexive pronoun agreeing with the subject, which — like the possessive/
interrogative determiners above — doesn't exist yet).
